# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people can work with. Disagree about the code as sharply as you like; do not make it
about the person.

## What is expected

- **Argue from evidence.** This project changes its mind when shown a measurement. "Your check is
  wrong, here is the page that proves it" is the most welcome sentence in the tracker.
- **Assume the other person is competent and busy.** Most maintainers here are one person with an
  agency to run.
- **Accept a no.** Not every feature belongs in a tool whose promise is that it has no dependencies
  and runs on shared hosting. A refusal comes with a reason; you may argue with the reason.
- **Say when you were wrong**, and move on without ceremony. The commit history of this project is
  full of it.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Comments demeaning someone for who they are rather than
what they wrote — this includes age, disability, appearance, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity or expression, level of experience, education, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation. Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained
disruption of a discussion. Sexual attention or advances of any kind.

Being technically right does not license any of it.

## Scope

Anywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and any space
where someone is representing UptimeEZ.

## Enforcement

Report to **support(@)uptimeEZ.com**. Reports are handled privately; the reporter's identity is
not disclosed to the person reported without their agreement.

Responses run from a private word, through an edit or removal of the content with a public reason,
to a temporary or permanent ban. Which one it is depends on severity and on whether it happened
before. If you think a decision was wrong, say so in the same thread you were told about it, once,
with your reasons.
